## Q1. Android Management API (AMAPI): terms, permissible usage, quotas, enterprise creation, cost (2024–2026 changes)

### Takeaway
AMAPI is technically the ideal "CSV → policy JSON → QR code" backend, but Google's Permissible Usage policy restricts it to **commercial EMM developers, Device Trust providers and OEMs**. Since late 2025, **new Google Cloud projects get a 0-device quota** until Google approves a business justification. Approval for up to 500 devices takes a justification form, and going beyond that needs Android Enterprise partner validation. An in-house or single-customer kitting tool built directly on AMAPI is therefore **at policy risk**: it may be refused, or it may have to be positioned and registered as a commercial EMM/solution. Using the API itself appears to be free.

### Cited Findings
- **Who may use it (official):** "The use of Google's Android Management API and associated SDK ("Service") are restricted solely to commercial Enterprise Mobility Management (EMM) developers, Device Trust from Android Enterprise (Device Trust) solution providers, and Original Equipment Manufacturers of Android devices (OEMs)." Some language versions omit OEMs. — [AMAPI Permissible Usage (official)](https://developers.google.com/android/management/permissible-usage)
- **Prohibited uses (official, per search summary):** device-financing solutions; solutions built only for device/user monitoring independent of enterprise management; reselling altered devices; solutions used only for first-party in-house apps. EMM developers must have a **direct agreement with end customers** and tell them how data is accessed and used. OEMs may use it only to enable EMM features that meet GMS requirements. — [AMAPI Permissible Usage](https://developers.google.com/android/management/permissible-usage)
- **Quotas (official):** "Android Management API has default quotas, including a limit on registered devices and queries per 100 seconds, which can be increased by request." Per the search summary of the same page: a default of **1,000 queries per 100 seconds per project**, a per-resource cap of **60 queries/minute**, and "To request an initial quota of up to 500 devices, you will need to provide a full business justification." Going beyond 500 requires applying through Android Enterprise (partner validation). — [AMAPI Permissible Usage](https://developers.google.com/android/management/permissible-usage)
- **Timeline of tightening (third-party, consistent across sources):**
  - **2024-03:** Bayton reports the Permissible Usage page changed from no explicit maximum to a **500-device maximum** per unvalidated project, now enforced by the API. — [bayton.org, "Google quietly introduces new quotas for unvalidated AMAPI use" (2024-03)](https://bayton.org/blog/2024/03/amapi-permissible-usage/)
  - **2024-12-16:** FileWave first observed unexpected 500-device enforcement. Its KB, last reviewed **2026-07-23**, still says Google documents a 500-device quota for standard projects and that an approved increase is needed to exceed it. — [FileWave KB: Android Enterprise 500-Device Enrollment Quota](https://kb.filewave.com/books/android/page/android-enterprise-500-device-enrollment-quota)
  - **2025-05:** "The use of FRP and factory reset policies will no longer be possible for Google Cloud Projects that have not been validated with the Android Enterprise team." — [bayton.org notes, 06 May 2025](https://bayton.org/notes/99/)
  - **2025-10-29 / 2025-11-01:** A community post says the policy was updated on 29 Oct 2025 from "default 500 devices" to "must request an initial quota of 500". Bayton reports that effective **1 November 2025**, Google requires Android Enterprise registration and approval before devices can enrol through AMAPI, and that **the default device quota for new projects is 0**. — [androidenterprise.community: Partner Application/Quota Status](https://www.androidenterprise.community/discussions/conversations/android-enterprise-partner-applicationquota-status/13543); [bayton.org FAQ: My AMAPI project returns a usage limit error](https://bayton.org/android/android-enterprise-faq/amapi-usage-limits/); [jasonbayton/11ty: amapi-device-quota-limits.md ("default quota of 0 devices per project")](https://github.com/jasonbayton/11ty/blob/main/_src/android/android-enterprise-faq/amapi-device-quota-limits.md)
  - Projects created in early 2025 or earlier "may only see this in relation to an unverified project exceeding 500 devices". The fix is to read, accept and submit the quota increase form on the Permissible Usage page. — [bayton.org FAQ](https://bayton.org/android/android-enterprise-faq/amapi-usage-limits/)
- **Symptom when unapproved:** enrollment fails with "Since your organization has reached its usage limits, this device can't be set up", even with zero devices enrolled. A reply: projects "are 0-quota by default and you must provide a business justification to Google to have your project granted any quota". Reported response times are 7+ business days, sometimes weeks. — [AE community: Org usage limits error, no enrolled devices](https://www.androidenterprise.community/android-enterprise-general-discussions-3/org-usage-limits-error-no-enrolled-devices-2661); [AE community: Device Owner Enrollment Error "Organization Has Reached Its Usage Limits" Even With Zero Devices](https://www.androidenterprise.community/android-enterprise-general-discussions-3/device-owner-enrollment-error-organization-has-reached-its-usage-limits-even-with-zero-devices-1562)
- **In-house use:** An AE community reply argues that "internal-only applications fall foul of this, as Google expects folks to build commercially available solutions". Bayton lists in-house solutions among the restricted cases and writes that the restrictions prevent building something yourself *for* yourself. In 2026 he also argued that Android may be the slowest platform to see AI-built in-house MDMs **because of AMAPI's permissible-usage rules**. — [AE community: Android Management API for internal use](https://www.androidenterprise.community/android-enterprise-general-discussions-3/android-management-api-for-internal-use-822); [bayton.org search summary](https://bayton.org/android/android-enterprise-faq/amapi-usage-limits/); [bayton.org, "MDM is dead. Long live ACE?" (2026-03)](https://bayton.org/blog/2026/03/mdm-is-dead-long-live-ace/)
- **Cost:** No official pricing page was found. Community answers say AMAPI "is completely free to use", and one adds that it is free "but it requires product validation and joining EMM community starting with a given number of devices". An unverified blog lists incidental Google Cloud costs (Pub/Sub notifications around $0.40 per million messages, logging). — [AE community thread](https://androidenterprise.community/discussions/conversations/android-management-api-for-internal-use/10034); [DEV Community (unverified)](https://dev.to/devops_fundamental/gcp-fundamentals-android-management-api-1eoc)
- **Enterprise creation / account types (official):** Google lists three ways to create an enterprise binding: **managed Google domain sign-up (recommended)**, **managed Google Play Accounts sign-up (deprecated)**, and managed Google domain enrollment. Managed Google Play Accounts give access only to managed Google Play. Managed Google domains can also provision managed Google Accounts. The AMAPI `enterprises` resource has a method that generates an **enterprise upgrade URL** for an existing managed Google Play Accounts enterprise. — [Play EMM API: Create an enterprise binding](https://developers.google.com/android/work/play/emm-api/create-enterprise); [AMAPI REST: enterprises](https://developers.google.com/android/management/reference/rest/v1/enterprises); [Play EMM API: Provision user accounts](https://developers.google.com/android/work/play/emm-api/user-accounts)
- **2026 release-note highlights (official, via search summary):** Feb 2026 — `privateDnsSettings` (company-owned devices on Android 10+) and a **preview of MCP (Model Context Protocol) integration**. H1 2026 — Android XR device management. Aug 2026 — credential-provider app controls (Android 14+) and more resilient work-profile enrollment. Q3 2026 — a new `backupService` policy field, and Android 6.0 devices no longer receive AMAPI updates but can still enroll. AMAPI SDK 1.8.3 (2026-08-12) raised the minimum API level to 24, and SDK 1.8.4 shipped on 2026-09-10. — [AMAPI release notes](https://developers.google.com/android/management/release-notes); [AE community: AMAPI H1 Update 2026](https://www.androidenterprise.community/product-updates/android-management-api-h1-update-2026-2789); [AMAPI SDK release notes](https://developers.google.com/android/management/sdk-release-notes); [AMAPI MCP tools reference](https://developers.google.com/android/management/reference/mcp/get_policy)
- **Third-party AMAPI tooling by a single developer:** "AMAPI Commander" (Feb 2026) is a read-only AI layer on Google's AMAPI MCP server. "Flash MDM" (Mar 2026) is a multi-tenant MDM on AMAPI built by one developer as a case study. — [bayton.org, Introducing AMAPI Commander (2026-02)](https://bayton.org/blog/2026/02/introducing-amapi-commander/); [bayton.org (2026-03)](https://bayton.org/blog/2026/03/mdm-is-dead-long-live-ace/)

### Inferences
- For the user's tool, the realistic AMAPI routes are: (1) register as a commercial EMM/solution with Google: build the tool as a product sold to customers under direct agreements, then pass partner validation for >500 devices; (2) the customer or SIer uses an existing commercial EMM built on AMAPI, and the tool drives that EMM's API instead of AMAPI directly; or (3) apply with a business justification for ≤500 devices and accept the risk that it is refused as an "internal-only" use. Plan for weeks of lead time and for refusal.
- Unvalidated projects lost FRP/factory-reset policies in May 2025, which hurts kitting/de-kitting (wipe and FRP handling are core lifecycle steps), so validation is effectively mandatory for production use.
- The tightening is **monotonic** (2024: 500 cap → 2025-05: feature limits → 2025-11: 0 default). Treat further restriction as likely when assessing tier (d) risk.
- AMAPI **is MDM in every practical sense**: Google's Android Device Policy becomes the Device Owner, and the device keeps checking in to Google's cloud. A customer that forbids MDM would reject it.
- **PowerShell automation:** AMAPI is plain REST/JSON (`androidmanagement.googleapis.com/v1/...`), so PowerShell 5.1 `Invoke-RestMethod` works once it has an OAuth2 access token. The hard part on 5.1 is signing the service-account JWT (RS256 with a PKCS#8 key). The simplest workaround is to shell out to `gcloud auth print-access-token` or use a small helper. (Inference; the REST shape is from training knowledge.)

- I could not read the **current verbatim text** of the Permissible Usage page (the domain was blocked) to confirm (a) the exact effective date of the 0-default quota, (b) whether "enterprises building their own solution" is explicitly excluded, and (c) the current partner-validation form URL. This needs a direct read before any go/no-go.
- No official AMAPI pricing statement was found. "Free" rests on community statements.
- The detailed 2024–2025 change from managed Google Play Accounts to Google-account-based enterprise sign-up (e.g., signup URLs that accept any work email, and dates) was not confirmed beyond "managed Google Play Accounts sign-up = deprecated" and the existence of the upgrade-URL method.
- No source said whether Google approves **SIer/kitting-vendor** tools (pre-enrollment staging for customers) as "commercial EMM".

## Q2. AMAPI kitting capabilities: enrollment tokens / QR, policy JSON, commands, inventory, compliance-as-verification, private apps / APKs, AOSP

### Takeaway
Feature-wise, AMAPI covers most of what fabriq's Windows modules do. A per-device or per-batch enrollment token yields a QR code. The policy JSON covers apps (force-install, block, kiosk), managed configs, Wi-Fi including EAP (via an ONC subset), system-update windows, password rules and kiosk customisation. The device resource provides inventory and compliance read-back. Remote commands cover lock, reboot, password reset, lost mode and eSIM. Since AMAPI SDK 1.6.0 (2025), it can install private APKs directly (custom apps). It depends on Google Play services / Android Device Policy, so it does **not** fit AOSP/non-GMS devices.

### Cited Findings
- **Enrollment tokens (official):** `duration` ranges from 1 minute to about 10,000 years (`Durations.MAX_VALUE`). The default is 1 hour if unset. The maximum was raised to ~10,000 years in **October 2022** (earlier docs say 30/90 days). `oneTimeOnly=true` lets only one device register. `qrCode` is "a JSON string whose UTF-8 bytes can be turned into a QR code for enrolling a device". For NFC, the record must contain a serialized `java.util.Properties` of the JSON's properties. `allowPersonalUsage` gained `PERSONAL_USAGE_DISALLOWED_USERLESS` for fully managed devices (**May 2024**). `get`/`list` now return the populated `value`, `qrCode` and `allowPersonalUsage`. — [AMAPI REST: enterprises.enrollmentTokens](https://developers.google.com/android/management/reference/rest/v1/enterprises.enrollmentTokens); [AMAPI release notes](https://developers.google.com/android/management/release-notes)
- **Device resource / inventory (official):** a Device "includes details on its state, policy, compliance, software, hardware, applications, network, memory, power, and security". `hardwareInfo` carries detailed hardware information. Some data depends on the policy's `statusReportingSettings`; for example, hardware status samples are available only if `hardwareStatusEnabled` is true. Timestamps include `lastStatusReportTime` and `lastPolicyComplianceReportTime`. — [AMAPI REST: enterprises.devices](https://developers.google.com/android/management/reference/rest/v1/enterprises.devices); [Ruby client Device docs](https://googleapis.dev/ruby/google-api-client/latest/Google/Apis/AndroidmanagementV1/Device.html)
- **Commands (official):** `devices.issueCommand` returns an Operation. LOCK, RESET_PASSWORD, REBOOT and RELINQUISH_OWNERSHIP take no extra parameters; RESET_PASSWORD can carry an optional new password and flags. On Android 14 a numeric password must be ≥6 characters or the command fails with INVALID_VALUE. START_LOST_MODE requires at least one of message/phone/email/address, and STOP_LOST_MODE ends it. **ADD_ESIM** (Android 15+) takes an activation code; ACTIVATED state is allowed only on company-owned devices, and the command returns the ICCID. — [AMAPI REST: devices.issueCommand](https://developers.google.com/android/management/reference/rest/v1/enterprises.devices/issueCommand); [AMAPI Lost Mode guide](https://developers.google.com/android/management/lost-mode)
- REBOOT is reported as supported only on fully managed devices (third-party). — [fleetdm/fleet issue #23232](https://github.com/fleetdm/fleet/issues/23232)
- **Wi-Fi / network (official):** Wi-Fi networks go in the policy's `openNetworkConfiguration` field, which supports only a **subset** of the ONC spec; NetworkConfiguration needs GUID, Name, Type and WiFi. The docs include an EAP-TLS example using `ServerCARefs` and `ClientCertKeyPairAlias`; on Android 12+ the key pair with that alias is granted Wi-Fi authentication access. Supported `Outer` values: EAP-AKA, EAP-TLS, EAP-TTLS, EAP-SIM, EAP-PWD, PEAP. `wifiConfigDisabled` blocks manual Wi-Fi configuration on fully managed devices; setting it without any ONC networks disables Wi-Fi access entirely. `MinimumWifiSecurityLevel` can, for example, block open networks. — [AMAPI: Configure networks](https://developers.google.com/android/management/configure-networks); [AMAPI REST: enterprises.policies](https://developers.google.com/android/management/reference/rest/v1/enterprises.policies)
- **Certificate caveat (third-party):** a community thread reports that certificates pushed through ONC did not appear in the keychain, and suggests a delegated cert-installer app. This may be dated. — [AE community: Unable to install certificates for WiFi](https://www.androidenterprise.community/android-enterprise-general-discussions-3/unable-to-install-certificates-for-wifi-1347?postid=4804)
- **Other policy types (official schema):** `SystemUpdate`/`SystemUpdateType`, `FreezePeriod`, `KioskCustomization`, `SetupAction`, `LaunchAppAction`, plus radio enums such as `UltraWidebandState` and `CellularTwoGState`. — [AMAPI REST: enterprises.policies](https://developers.google.com/android/management/reference/rest/v1/enterprises.policies)
- **Direct APK install (2025):** "With the release of AMAPI SDK 1.6.0-rc01, Google has introduced long-awaited support for direct APK installation via the Android Management API." Google documents "Manage custom apps with AMAPI" for **fully managed devices**, via an extensibility app built on AMAPI SDK ≥1.6.0-rc01. — [bayton.org, "AMAPI finally supports direct APK installation" (2025-08)](https://bayton.org/blog/2025/08/amapi-apk-deployment/); [AMAPI: Manage custom apps](https://developers.google.com/android/management/manage-custom-apps)
- **Private apps via managed Google Play (official):** the Custom App Publishing API publishes private apps visible only to the enterprise. Managed Google Play also supports **externally hosted private apps**. — [Play Custom App Publishing API](https://developers.google.com/android/work/play/custom-app-api); [Managed Google Play Help: Externally hosted private apps](https://support.google.com/googleplay/work/answer/6145182?hl=en); [AMAPI: Distribute apps](https://developers.google.com/android/management/apps)
- **AOSP / non-GMS:** I found no Google documentation for AMAPI on AOSP devices. Vendors say non-GMS rugged devices need their own Device Owner agent and an APK upload path instead. — [Applivery AOSP docs](https://docs.applivery.com/en/device-management/android/aosp/); [Esper: managed config without Google Play](https://www.esper.io/blog/esper-gms-aosp-managed-config)

### Inferences
- **CSV → AMAPI mapping (design idea):** a `hostlist.csv` row becomes one `enrollmentTokens.create` call with `oneTimeOnly=true`, the row's `policyName`, and the host ID in the token's `additionalData` (training knowledge, not re-verified). The tool then renders `qrCode` as a PNG for printing or display. A profile CSV module list becomes one policy JSON (each module maps to a policy fragment, e.g., `applications[]`, `openNetworkConfiguration`, `systemUpdate`, `passwordPolicies`). Verification (Step 5.5) means polling `devices.get` and checking `policyCompliant`, `nonComplianceDetails` and `appliedPolicyVersion`, which are standard Device fields (training knowledge, not re-verified). Evidence means dumping `hardwareInfo` (serial) and `networkInfo` (IMEI/MEID; training knowledge) into the HTML checklist. This matches fabriq's idempotent apply + read-back model closely, and is arguably cleaner than ADB because the policy is declarative.
- `installType` values I recall for `applications[]` include FORCE_INSTALLED, BLOCKED, KIOSK, REQUIRED_FOR_SETUP, PREINSTALLED and AVAILABLE, plus `managedConfiguration` and `deviceRadioState` (wifiState/airplaneMode etc.). This is training knowledge and was **not re-verified** this session because the policy reference page could not be fetched.
- Fit risk: none of this matters unless Q1's permissible-usage and quota hurdle is cleared.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm the exact field names and semantics of `deviceRadioState`, `setupActions`, `kioskCustomization`, `passwordPolicies`, `HardwareInfo.serialNumber` / `NetworkInfo.imei`, `policyCompliant`, or the WIPE command / `devices.delete` `wipeDataFlags` (the reference pages could not be fetched).
- I found no source on AMAPI's **migration** features (e.g., moving devices from a custom DPC to Android Device Policy without a wipe). Bayton has a 2026-04 post on DPC migration that I could not read. — [bayton.org, DPC migration (2026-04)](https://bayton.org/blog/2026/04/dpc-migration/)
- I could not confirm whether the custom-app (APK) path needs managed Google Play for the extensibility app (likely, by inference), or whether it works on non-GMS devices (likely not).

## Q3. Managed Google Play and the custom-DPC landscape (affects tiers b/c and zero-touch with a self-made DPC)

### Takeaway
Google has closed the old route for a self-made DPC to integrate with managed Google Play: the Play EMM API accepts no new registrations, and AMAPI is the recommended path. Since 2025, **Google Play Protect blocks unlisted custom DPCs during provisioning**, while Android Device Policy (AMAPI) is always allowlisted. A self-made Device Owner app (tiers b/c) therefore faces an approval hurdle of its own, separate from AMAPI quotas.

### Cited Findings
- "We are no longer accepting new registrations for the Play EMM API." New EMM solutions should use AMAPI with Google's Android Device Policy DPC. Google continues to support existing EMM community members with validated custom DPCs. — [Play EMM API overview (official)](https://developers.google.com/android/work/play/emm-api); [Register for the EMM Community (official)](https://developers.google.com/android/work/play/emm-api/register)
- AMAPI "reduces development effort by eliminating the need for custom DPCs" (official framing). — [Android Developers: Build a DPC](https://developer.android.com/work/dpc/build-dpc)
- Several Play EMM API methods used by custom-DPC vendors were deprecated in Sep 2021 and turned off on **2025-09-30** (third-party; not confirmed against Google's pages). — [jasonbayton/11ty: provisioning methods](https://github.com/jasonbayton/11ty/blob/main/_src/android/android-enterprise-provisioning-methods.md); [bayton.org: provisioning methods](https://bayton.org/android/android-enterprise-provisioning-methods/)
- **Play Protect DPC allowlist (2025):** Play Protect checks new DPCs against Google's approved list during enrolment and blocks unlisted ones with "Harmful app blocked", even legitimate ones. Android Device Policy is always on the list. Developers can file a Play Protect appeal, but there are no published timelines. Some developers report that removing sensitive permissions (notification listener, SMS) allowed QR install; others report blocks even after minimal rebuilds. — [bayton.org, "Google Play Protect is now the custom DPC gatekeeper…" (2025-12)](https://bayton.org/blog/2025/12/the-dpc-allowlist/); [bayton.org glossary](https://bayton.org/android/android-glossary/); [AE community: Play Protect blocking custom DPC during 6-tap QR provisioning](https://www.androidenterprise.community/android-enterprise-general-discussions-3/play-protect-blocking-custom-dpc-app-during-6-tap-qr-provisioning-appeal-submitted-2909); [AE community: Play Protect blocking custom DPC apps](https://www.androidenterprise.community/android-enterprise-general-discussions-3/play-protect-blocking-custom-dpc-apps-how-to-get-approval-or-alternatives-1271)

### Inferences
- In 2026 Google is funneling every **GMS** Device Owner use case through either (i) AMAPI/Android Device Policy, gated by permissible usage and quota, or (ii) an allowlisted, validated custom DPC. Neither gate is designed for a one-off in-house kitting tool. Tier (b) (ADB `dpm set-device-owner` with a self-made app) may sidestep the QR-time Play Protect check, but that was not verified here and belongs to the other researchers' scope.
- Managed Google Play (needed for private apps through Play) is tied to an Android Enterprise binding, which in turn needs an EMM or AMAPI. A no-MDM project therefore cannot easily use managed Play private apps.

### Gaps
- I found no official Google statement of the allowlist criteria, or of whether ADB-set Device Owners are also checked.
- I could not verify the exact 2025-09-30 Play EMM API turn-off list.

## Q4. Zero-touch enrollment (ZTE): requirements, assignment, self-made DPC, Japanese resellers, AER devices

### Takeaway
ZTE is free to customers but **only for devices bought from an authorized zero-touch reseller**. The reseller registers devices (by IMEI/serial) to the customer's zero-touch account; customers cannot add devices themselves. The customer then assigns a configuration naming a DPC (Android Device Policy for AMAPI EMMs, or a vendor custom DPC) plus DPC-extras JSON. ZTE is only a **launcher into an MDM**, so it is useless without one and always counts as "MDM". In Japan, SoftBank, KDDI, NTT docomo business, T-Gaia and distributors such as Daiwabo handle ZTE, and Sharp, FCNT and Kyocera business models carry Android Enterprise Recommended (AER).

### Cited Findings
- **Prerequisites (official):** a zero-touch account created by an authorized reseller, plus an EMM that supports company-owned devices. Google recommends 2-Step Verification on the admin Google account. Admins sign in with the Google account they gave the reseller. Devices must be bought from a reseller or Google partner, not retail. Zero-touch is blocked on some devices because of software incompatibilities. — [Android Enterprise Help: Zero-touch enrollment for IT admins (EN)](https://support.google.com/work/android/answer/7514005?hl=en); [same (JA)](https://support.google.com/work/android/answer/7514005?hl=ja); [Android Enterprise Help: Get Android devices (JA)](https://support.google.com/work/android/answer/15479218?hl=JA)
- **Device registration:** "Only resellers can add devices. Customers are not able to add devices to the zero-touch portal at this time" (third-party; may predate the latest portal update). The reseller must register devices to the customer's ZTE portal. — [bayton.org: What is Android zero-touch enrolment?](https://bayton.org/android/what-is-android-zero-touch-enrolment/); [Omnissa docs: Enroll Android Device Using Zero-Touch](https://docs.omnissa.com/android-device-management-VSaaS/AndroidEnrollmentEnrollZEROTOUCH)
- **Configuration / API (official):** the zero-touch customer API `customers.configurations` includes `dpcExtras` (JSON EMM provisioning extras passed to the DPC) and a DPC resource `customers/[CUSTOMER_ID]/dpcs/*`; `customers.dpcs.list` lists the supported DPCs. Google recommends setting a **default configuration** for newly purchased devices. — [Zero-touch customer API: customers.configurations](https://developers.google.com/zero-touch/reference/customer/rest/v1/customers.configurations); [Zero-touch EMM integration guide](https://developers.google.com/zero-touch/guides/customer/emm)
- **Zero-touch iframe (official):** AMAPI-based EMM consoles can embed a zero-touch iframe to link zero-touch accounts and set an enterprise default profile; devices without their own zero-touch profile get that default. — [AMAPI: Zero-touch iframe](https://developers.google.com/android/management/zero-touch-iframe)
- **DPC choice:** for AMAPI-based EMMs the DPC is Android Device Policy. Custom-DPC vendors supply their own DPC and vendor-specific extras. A pasted full QR payload "may function, but it's not best practice". — [bayton.org: zero-touch DPC extras collection](https://bayton.org/android/android-enterprise-zero-touch-dpc-extras-collection/); [bayton.org ZT generator](https://bayton.org/zt-generator/)
- **Japan, carriers/resellers:**
  - SoftBank builds ZTE into its MDM "BCDM" kitting: devices are pre-linked to the MDM, and enrollment completes within a few taps after power-on. — [SoftBank BCDM: Android Enterprise](https://www.softbank.jp/biz/services/security/bcdm/function/android/)
  - KDDI offers a paid "Android Enterprise サポート" outsourcing service, and its Smart Mobile Safety Manager kitting manual (updated **2026-06-25**) has a section "3.7.2 ゼロタッチ登録(Android Enterprise 企業アカウント)でセットアップする". KDDI also offers EMM kitting on behalf of customers for business arrows models. — [KDDI Android Enterprise support](https://biz.kddi.com/service/gyoumushien/android-enterprise-support/); [KDDI SMSM Android kitting manual PDF](https://smsmsupport-manual.smartmanager.jp/pdf/Android_Kitting.pdf); [KDDI arrows We FCG01 (business)](https://biz.kddi.com/service/mobile/product/fcg01/)
  - NTT docomo business (ntt.com) says it provides AER-compliant devices and services, including zero-touch support. — [ntt.com: 法人向け製品](https://www.ntt.com/business/mobile/product.html); [ntt.com: ドコモスマホ×Android Enterprise](https://www.ntt.com/business/lp/android-enterprise.html)

## Q8. Cross-cutting: "Is it MDM?", accounts/contracts/cost, USB debugging, what stays manual, fit with a fabriq-like CSV tool

- **Still manual on every platform:** SIM insertion and carrier line activation/APN (unless eSIM via AMAPI ADD_ESIM on Android 15+ company-owned devices); physical asset labels and IMEI/serial ↔ label reconciliation; screenshot or photo evidence of the final state (AMAPI/MDM inventory can substitute part of it); the initial network join for QR flows (the AMAPI QR payload can usually embed Wi-Fi provisioning extras; training knowledge, not re-verified); Google account sign-in for any user-facing Google services when managed Google accounts are not used; carrier-specific setup screens (Knox Configure can skip them on Galaxy).
- **Recommended framing for the report writer:** tier (d) is viable mainly as **"the tool drives a customer-chosen commercial EMM, or OEM no-MDM staging tools"**, not as "the tool talks to AMAPI directly". The direct-AMAPI path needs Google's commercial-EMM approval, which since Nov 2025 starts at a 0-device quota. For no-MDM customers (like the earlier Mac project), only OEM offline stagers (StageNow/Honeywell EP/Kyocera DC) and possibly **Knox Configure Setup edition** come close, and each is single-vendor.
